Privacy Notice

Last updated 12 July 2026 · applies to the Paper Trail desktop app and this website

The short version. Paper Trail keeps your data on your machine. There's no account, no analytics, and no server of ours holding your manuscripts or submission history. Paper Trail does not send your manuscript titles, manuscript IDs, co-author names, journal statuses or journal passwords to us. The app communicates directly with journal websites to retrieve information. Our download, update and community-statistics providers may receive ordinary technical connection data, as explained below.

Who we are

Paper Trail is made by J. Adam Carter, Glasgow, United Kingdom. For anything in this notice, or to exercise any of the rights described in it, write to adam.carter@glasgow.ac.uk. For the purposes of the UK GDPR and the EU GDPR, the controller for the small amount of processing described below is J. Adam Carter.

What stays on your machine

  • Your tracked papers: titles, journals, co-authors, statuses, submission dates, downloaded manuscripts and journal covers. These are stored in the app's local data folder and nowhere else. Deleting the app's data removes them.
  • Your journal logins: encrypted into the operating system's secure storage (the Mac Keychain, or the Windows equivalent). They are used only to sign in to the journals you choose, from your computer. We never receive them and could not read them if we wanted to.

What leaves your machine, and to whom

  • Journal websites. When you add a paper or click Refresh, the app connects to the relevant journal's submission system directly from your computer, exactly as your browser would, using the login you provided. That traffic goes between you and the journal; it does not pass through us.
  • GitHub (downloads and updates). The installers are hosted on GitHub, and the app checks GitHub for new versions. GitHub receives ordinary connection data (your IP address, user agent and the request) under GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Publisher pages (covers and journal statistics). The app fetches journal cover images and published acceptance/wait-time figures from publisher websites. Those sites receive ordinary connection data from your machine.
  • Community statistics (only if you opt in). Described in full in the next section. The receiving service runs on Cloudflare, which processes ordinary connection data under Cloudflare's privacy policy.

That's the complete list. The app contains no analytics, no crash reporting and no advertising identifiers.

Community statistics (opt-in)

Paper Trail can contribute to shared journal statistics, so that figures like "median days to first decision" come from real submissions rather than folklore. This is off by default. If, and only if, you switch it on, the app sends one record when a tracked paper reaches a decision. The record, in full:

{"v":1,"journal":"mind","outcome":"rejected","daysToDecision":165,"month":"2026-07","app":"0.2.2"}

It carries no name, no manuscript title, no manuscript ID and no precise dates: days are rounded, and only the month is kept. The record is anonymous when it leaves your machine, and the receiving service stores nothing about the request itself; there is no account or identifier to tie a record to a person. Aggregated figures are only ever published once enough records exist for a journal that no individual submission could be picked out.

You can switch community contributions off at any time; this prevents all future contributions. Once a record has been transmitted and irreversibly anonymised, we cannot identify it as yours and therefore cannot retrieve or delete it individually. Because the records are anonymous, they are not personal data under the UK and EU GDPR; consent in the app is asked for all the same, because we think you should decide.

This website

This site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which receives ordinary connection data when you visit. The site itself sets no cookies and runs no analytics. The download buttons query GitHub's public API from your browser to find the latest version.

Your rights

Under the UK and EU GDPR you have rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection over personal data we hold about you. In practice we hold almost none: your papers and logins are on your machine, under your control, and the community records are anonymous. If you email us, we'll hold your correspondence for as long as the conversation needs. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) or your local supervisory authority.

Changes

If the app's data practices change (for instance, if community statistics ever grow new fields), this notice will be updated first and the change will be called out in the app's release notes. The date at the top tells you the version you're reading.
