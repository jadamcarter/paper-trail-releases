A paper's life

A paper doesn't end at "submitted".

Most tools help with one stage and lose interest at the next. Paper Trail follows the whole arc — and the parts nobody builds for are the parts it takes most seriously.

Write itthe months nobody sees

An editor that knows what a paper is

Plain Markdown files in your own folder — nothing locked in a database. Type @ to cite straight from Zotero. Cut a paragraph you can't quite bin and it waits in a drawer beside the paper. Keep the plan, the objections and the seminar's pushback in a notebook that opens next to the prose. Hear it read back to you before you send it.

Send itone afternoon, done properly

The submission pack, built for you

Pick the journal and it assembles what that journal asks for: the anonymised manuscript in every format required, a separate title page, the house citation style, the publisher's own LaTeX class where one exists. It checks the word limit under the journal's own counting rule before you go anywhere near the portal.

Then waitthe bit that eats your attention

It checks the portals so you stop checking them

Paper Trail signs in to the journal's system and reads the status itself, quietly, several times an hour. You are told when something changes — not invited to go and look. Each paper keeps a trail of what happened and when, so three weeks of silence looks like three weeks of silence, and you can see how long this journal usually takes.

Revise itwhere the real work is

A desk for the revision

The referees' points become a checklist you answer one at a time, so nothing is quietly skipped. It keeps the version you submitted alongside the one you are writing and builds the "changes marked" copy the journal asks for — the document everyone otherwise assembles by hand at midnight.

Publish itand the clock nobody remembers

Proofs, deposit, and the open-access deadline

Acceptance starts a second, quieter set of obligations: proofs to return, a repository deposit with a hard deadline attached to it. Paper Trail counts those days for you and says so before they run out, because that one is not recoverable afterwards.

The other desks

And the rest of the week.

Your own papers are perhaps a third of the job. The rest has no home anywhere else.

Refereeing

The reports you owe, with their deadlines, the manuscript to hand, and the review written and kept right there. A yearly tally for when someone asks what you did.

Editing

If you edit a journal, it walks your queues and shows each manuscript with its referees — who agreed, who declined, who is overdue and by how many days. No guessing, no clicking through.

Reading for others

Comments you owe a colleague or a student: their draft, annotated in the app, sent back by email. The part of the year that otherwise leaves no record at all.

Grants

Applications with their funders, deadlines, co-applicants and portals — and the papers that came out of the money, linked back to it.

Books

A monograph is not a long paper. Chapters, a delivery date, and whole-book export through Quarto in a proper book design.

Talks & slides

Where you are speaking and when, the deck written beside the paper it came from, and what they said afterwards.

The record

December's forms, already written.

Every year the same request arrives in a different shape: an annual review, a promotion case, a REF return, a funder's conflict-of-interest list. All of it is work you already did.

Your year, in prose

Submissions, decisions, refereeing, editing, the drafts you read for other people — written out as sentences you can paste into the form.

The publication list

A formatted bibliography, BibTeX, or a spreadsheet. Bring in everything you published before you started, from ORCID or a BibTeX file, and it covers your whole career.

Service, remembered

The viva in March, the tenure letter in June, the committee you sit on. Ten seconds to write down, and the December form fills itself.

Collaborators

The recent-collaborator list funders ask for, worked out from your co-authors, co-applicants and the issues you edited.

Your tools

It meets your tools where they are.

Zotero

Cite with @ as you write. Read papers in Zotero's own reader, where your highlights belong — then pull those highlights into the draft as quotations, with their page.

Your calendar

Revision deadlines, expected decisions and ready-by dates written into the calendar you already look at, so they reach your phone.

GitHub

Optional: every save of every draft committed and pushed to a private repository you own. The full history of your writing, safe off the machine.

Plain files

Your drafts are Markdown in your own folder, syncing through iCloud like everything else. If you stopped using Paper Trail tomorrow, every paper would still be there.

Your data

Your work stays on your machine.

This is a tool for unpublished research and confidential peer review, so the default had to be the strict one.

No account, no server

There is nothing to sign up for. Your papers, drafts and notes live in your own folders and are never uploaded anywhere by the app.

Journal logins stay local

Saved sign-ins are encrypted by your operating system's own keychain and used only to talk to that journal, from your computer.

Nothing shared unless you ask

You can opt in to contributing anonymous decision timings — a journal, an outcome, a rounded number of days. Never a title, never a name. It is off until you turn it on.
