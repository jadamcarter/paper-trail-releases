Know where every paper stands.

Paper Trail is a desktop app that signs in to your journals and reads each manuscript's status straight off the dashboard. Everything ends up on one page: where each paper stands and when a decision is plausibly due, plus the polite way to chase the one that's overdue.

Stop making the rounds of journal dashboards.

ScholarOne logs you out more or less as a matter of principle. So checking becomes a ritual: sign in, squint at a table, sign in somewhere else. Paper Trail runs the ritual for you.

1

Track a paper

Type the title as it appears on the journal's site and pick the journal. The app finds the cover, the manuscript ID, and the submission date on its own.

2

Add your journal login, once

Each paper's card asks for its journal login the first time. It's encrypted into your device's own secure storage, and it stays on your machine.

3

Click “Refresh all”

The app signs in, reads every status, and redraws each paper's trail. If anything has changed since you last looked, you'll get a notification.

The questions are always the same.

How long has it been? When should I expect to hear? Is this journal slow, or is it me? And, after bad news: where next? Each card has an answer.

The trail

A living timeline

Submission to review to decision, drawn as one line. The marker pulses gently while reviewers have the paper, sits still through the administrative longueurs, and turns green, amber, or red when the decision lands.

Expected decisions

An honest countdown

Your submission date plus the journal's average review time gives a date you might plausibly hear by. The card counts down to it. When the average comes and goes, it counts up instead, in red. (It happens.)

Journal facts

Sourced, not folklore

Acceptance rates and wait times are mostly folklore, passed along at conferences. The card shows published figures instead: publisher metrics where journals release them, community surveys where they don't, side by side when they disagree, each linked to its source.

Where next?

Plan the resubmission

After a rejection there's a question you want answered while the formatting is still fresh: where next? Rank the plausible journals by decision speed or by selectivity, filtered to your word count. Anywhere that's already said no is greyed out.

The nudge

A polite status inquiry

Well past the average with no word? One click drafts the courteous chase-up email, manuscript ID and dates included, for you to edit and send. Nobody enjoys writing these, which is presumably why we put them off.

The journey

Every paper's biography

Papers get around. When one is rejected and resubmitted, the rejection carries forward, so the card shows the full route: where the paper has been, and how long each stop took.

There's also: co-author bylines, a word-limit checker for 25+ philosophy journals, CSV export of your submission history, decision dates as a calendar feed, and automatic updates.

Your data stays on your machine.

Paper Trail is a desktop app in the old-fashioned sense. There's no account to create and no server holding your submission history.

  • Your logins never leave your device. Journal passwords are encrypted into the operating system's secure storage (the Mac Keychain, or Windows's equivalent) and used only to sign in from your computer, the way you would.
  • Nothing is stored anywhere else. Statuses go from the journal's page to your screen, and that's the whole journey. Delete the app and it's all gone.
  • Community data is opt-in, and you can read it first. If, and only if, you switch it on, each concluded submission contributes one anonymous record to shared journal statistics. The record is reproduced here in full; there is nothing else.

Paper Trail does not send your manuscript titles, manuscript IDs, co-author names, journal statuses or journal passwords to us. The app communicates directly with journal websites to retrieve information. Our download, update and community-statistics providers may receive ordinary technical connection data, as explained in the Privacy Notice.

The entire record, verbatim
{"v":1,"journal":"mind","outcome":"rejected","daysToDecision":165,"month":"2026-07","app":"0.2.2"}
It carries no name and no title, and no precise dates either: days are rounded, and only the month is kept. Off by default.
