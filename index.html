An academic work studio — from the first note to the proofs

The whole life of a paper, in one place.

Write it, send it, wait for it, revise it, resubmit it. Paper Trail is an academic work studio: a quiet editor for the writing itself, a tracker that signs in to your journals and reads each status without being asked, and a revision desk that builds your reply to the referees and the marked-up copy the journal wants. One app, on your own machine, for the parts of the job that nobody teaches you to keep track of.

A paper doesn't end at "submitted".

It starts as a note to yourself and ends, years later, in proofs — and most of what happens in between is invisible to every tool you own. Paper Trail follows one paper through all of it, and keeps the other obligations of the week beside it.

Write it.

Not a text box bolted onto a tracker. A writing studio: plain Markdown files in your own iCloud Drive, a quiet page to write them on, and pandoc underneath — so what comes out is a properly typeset paper with its citations resolved and its references built.

The page

Somewhere calm to think

One centred column on warm paper, the markdown marks fading as you type. Typewriter scrolling and a focus mode keep the current paragraph under your eyes; ⛶ takes it truly full screen. ⌘B and ⌘I do what you expect, lists carry their markers down, ⌥⌘F writes a footnote and renumbers the paper around it — and ⌘Z still reaches back through every one of them. Six committed looks, from warm paper to green-phosphor terminal, each restyling the whole editor.

Lenses over the page

See what you've actually written

Switch on a lens and the page colours what you asked about and nothing else: adverbs, hedges and intensifiers, passive constructions, nominalizations, filler, a word used twice in one paragraph. Sentences shade as they pass thirty words, and again at forty-five. Colour only — nothing moves, nothing reflows, the caret stays where it was.

One lens is on by default, because the mistake it catches is silent: a citation with a full stop welded to it — @williams2020. — inside a footnote. Pandoc renders that as nothing and drops its reference from the bibliography without a warning. Paper Trail underlines it in red as you type.

Everything open at once

The paper beside the paper

Write and read the typeset version side by side, scrolling together. Open the paper you're replying to in a reading pane in the same window, instead of a PDF covering what you're writing. A rail down the side lists your sections with a word count on each — a four-thousand-word limit is a structural problem, and this is where you see which section caused it — or every work you've cited, with a warning on any citekey Zotero can no longer find.

Word counts follow each journal's own stated rule, read from what the journal actually says: Analysis's 4,000 includes the notes; Episteme's 12,000 includes the notes and the references. The footer names the journals your paper has just grown out of.

Nothing lost

Every hour of work, recoverable

Snapshots accumulate quietly as you write. History draws the paper's growth curve, compares any past version word by word, and will mark everything written since a chosen moment right there in the page — which is also how the marked-up copy for a resubmission gets made.

Drafts are ordinary .md files in iCloud Drive, so they sync between your Macs — which is why the editor watches for the other copy. If a paper has moved on since you opened it, the save is refused and you're shown both versions rather than quietly overwriting an afternoon's work.

Send it.

In house style, anonymized, checked

Open a starter document in the journal's own house style — title, abstract, headings, sample citations in its referencing style — as Word, LaTeX, Markdown or Quarto. Then wait, without checking.

ScholarOne logs you out more or less as a matter of principle, so checking becomes a ritual: sign in, squint at a table, sign in somewhere else, learn nothing. Paper Trail runs the ritual for you — and this is the part no other tool does.

1

Track a paper

Type the title as it appears on the journal's site and pick the journal. The app finds the cover, the manuscript ID and the submission date itself.

2

Add the login, once

Each card asks for its journal login the first time. It's encrypted into your device's own secure storage and never leaves the machine.

3

Click “Refresh all”

It signs in, reads every status, and redraws each paper's trail. If anything moved since you last looked, you're told — by notification, or by email if you'd rather.

What the card knows

An honest countdown, and its sources

A living timeline from submission to expected decision, with the stage it's actually at and how long each stage took. The expected date comes from real acceptance rates and reported wait times, each figure linked to where it came from — sourced, not folklore — so “36 days past average” means something. When it is genuinely late, the app drafts the status inquiry for you.

The whole record

Every paper's biography

Each paper keeps its own history: where it went, what came back, how long it took, which journals have declined it and when. The submitted PDF is filed alongside. When the decision is acceptance, the card runs the proofs clock — when they arrived, when corrections are due, the proofing link or the attached PDF one click away — beside a short production checklist: proofs returned, copyright form, online first, in print, each stamped with the date you ticked it. And when a paper's story is done, one click archives it: the record keeps, the desk clears.

Revise it.

A revise-and-resubmit is three documents, not one: the revised paper, a copy with the changes marked, and a reply that answers every point in the referees' own numbering. Paper Trail keeps the three together — and builds two of them for you.

The reply

Answer each point where you fix it

The decision letter arrives as a wall of text; Paper Trail splits it into the points it actually makes — the editor's letter, Referee A, Referee C — each tickable, each with room for the line you'd write back. Those lines are the reply. Tick the last one and the whole document is on your clipboard as LaTeX or Markdown, numbered exactly as the referees numbered themselves, ready to paste into Overleaf. Points you haven't answered come out plainly marked, so nothing goes back half-finished by accident.

The marked-up copy

Changes highlighted, without marking them by hand

Most journals want a copy showing what changed, and keeping one by hand is how resubmissions go wrong. Give Paper Trail the version you submitted and the version you're revising and it typesets one: additions underlined, deletions struck through. Drop an Overleaf Source zip and it finds the main document itself — bibliography, class file and figures included, because the comparison has to compile. A paper written in Paper Trail needs nothing dropped at all: the version you sent is already in its own history. Markdown, Quarto, LaTeX and Word all go through the same machinery.

And the rest of the week.

Submissions are the visible part of the job and not most of it. Seven tiles sit under your cards, each showing a live count and a quiet warning dot when something is getting close — so a glance at the strip is a glance at the week.

Writing

Every paper you're writing, one list

Start it here and it opens in the built-in editor, or attach a working copy that lives anywhere — a file, a folder, an Overleaf link — and it opens in whichever editor you chose. Word counts come off the file itself, against the journal you're aiming at. Books run at the same scale: attach the master file or the whole project folder, and chapters, contracts and delivery dates sit beside the papers.

In review

The trail, one tile up top

The namesake sits second on the strip: a live count of what's under review and the next expected decision, and a click drops you at the Submitted → Decision cards below — the app's own subject, never reachable only by scroll.

Owed

What you owe everyone else

Referee reports you've agreed to write, with the journal's own review platform one click away and its deadline in your calendar. And comments owed to colleagues and students: drop their paper in, mark it up in the app's own reader, and email it back with the annotated file attached.

Special issues

Issues you're editing

Special issues and edited volumes, where the contribution list is the whole feature: who said yes, who hasn't answered, what's arrived, what's finished — each contributor's deadline beside their name, and the next invitation one chip away.

Grants

Applications, idea to decision

Funder, scheme, deadline, the working copy, the portal — each application a card that moves from idea to submitted to a decision, with the deadline written into your calendar like everything else.

Plan

Where should this one go?

Every journal in one place — word limits, acceptance rates, typical decision times, each figure linked to its source. Pick one of your drafts, see where its length fits, grey out the journals that already said no, and set the target the editor counts against.

Notes

A scratch pad that files nothing

One page that's always there for the thought you don't want to lose and don't want to organise. Citations work here too. It saves itself and asks nothing of you.

It meets your tools where they are.

A tool like this earns its keep by talking to the things you already trust — your reference manager, your calendar, your typesetting. All of it runs on your machine.

Zotero

Your library, one @ away

Search your library from inside the app and open any paper in Zotero's own reader — highlights stay in Zotero, where they belong. Results tell you before you click whether the item actually has a PDF attached, and when it hasn't, one button looks for a legal open-access copy instead. Typing @ in a draft completes from your library and cites as you pick: Author (Year) while you read, the Better BibTeX key underneath. Paste a DOI and the reference is fetched into Zotero and cited on the spot.

And when the paper isn't in your library yet, an @ that finds nothing offers to go and look — a Crossref search in the same popover, which saves the paper into Zotero and cites it in the sentence you were already writing. Google Scholar and PhilPapers can't be searched from inside an app, so those open in your browser with the query filled in, where your own connector does the saving.

Calendar & typesetting

Deadlines where you'll see them

Every date the app knows — revision deadlines, expected decisions, referee reports, ready-by dates, grant deadlines — written into a calendar you choose and kept there: change a due date and its event moves, remove the paper and the event goes too. Pick a calendar that syncs and the dates reach your phone. Mac only for now; on Windows a one-click .ics export does the same by hand.

Typesetting runs on pandoc and your own LaTeX installation, so exports look like your papers rather than like an app's idea of a paper. The marked-up copy for a resubmission uses latexdiff, which comes with MacTeX and TeX Live.

Needs Zotero with the free Better BibTeX plugin for citations, and pandoc for exports — the app tells you plainly which of them it can find, and what still works when it can't.

Your data stays on your machine.

Paper Trail is a desktop app in the old-fashioned sense. There's no account to create and no server holding your submission history.

  • Your logins never leave your device. Journal passwords are encrypted into the operating system's secure storage (the Mac Keychain, or Windows's equivalent) and used only to sign in from your computer, the way you would.
  • Nothing is stored on anyone else's server. Your tracking data is one plain JSON file, and your drafts are ordinary Markdown files — both kept in your own iCloud Drive when you have it (so a second Mac just works), on your disk when you don't. Delete the app and everything is still yours, readable, in Finder.
  • Community data is opt-in, and you can read it first. If, and only if, you switch it on, each concluded submission contributes one anonymous record to shared journal statistics. The record is reproduced here in full; there is nothing else.

Paper Trail does not send your manuscript titles, manuscript IDs, co-author names, journal statuses or journal passwords to us. The app communicates directly with journal websites to retrieve information, and with doi.org only when you paste a DOI to import a reference. Our download, update and community-statistics providers may receive ordinary technical connection data, as explained in the Privacy Notice.
